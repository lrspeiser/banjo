## Code delivered together

**Fracture law:** `CohesiveFacet` distributes traction over three quadrature points on a triangular interface. Each point retains irreversible effective-opening history. Normal/tangential stiffness, strength and fracture energy are numeric SI inputs; optional shear couples through a declared effective-opening potential. Compression remains reversible and does not initiate tensile damage. Equal critical work across opening/shear is a model assumption, not calibrated glass or tissue data.

The tests independently integrate endpoint traction in pure opening and shear to the declared `Gc * area`, differentiate the undamaged potential, check unloading and mixed-direction paths, and verify reference-frame force/moment and rotation covariance. Algebraic work-conjugate forces are separate from endpoint forces; their exact discrete work identity does not certify energy conservation in a future time integrator.

**Separation and cutting foundation:** `FractureTopology` compiles matching internal facets and tetrahedron-local vertex copies while preserving physical mass, volume and constraints. `CohesiveAssembly` joins that topology to the fracture law, assembles actual nodal forces/history, and derives components/exposed faces only when all three integration points of a facet have separated. Partial damage preserves connectivity. Tests cover surviving alternate paths, heterogeneous laws, corrupted topology, bounded work and rejected-candidate state preservation. This is shared separation machinery; a blade and tomato simulation still need tool contact and an appropriate soft-material law.

**Fire/thermal acceptance:** `banjo_thermal_burn_acceptance` runs the existing sparse thermal runtime on matched four-cell glass/oak/iron demonstrators. It records actual temperature/fuel/oxygen histories, numeric properties/provenance, mass/products, chemical and sensible energy, external heater work, accepted clocks and work/backlog. Runs at 0.01 and 0.05 seconds per step both reach 0.1 seconds. A finite 3000 J heater pulse is identical across materials; these are explicit demonstration coefficients, not material calibration.

At 0.1 seconds the heated oak cell has released 89.6 J from its finite chemical reservoir and exhausted its oxygen. Glass and iron release no chemical heat. All three conduct heat to the adjacent cell; the isolated unheated control stays at 300 K. Maximum absolute combined energy residual is about 7.28e-12 J and mass residual is zero in these cases. Separate tests exhaust fuel with oxygen remaining, preserve response under renaming, and show a one-job cap leaves 0.24 seconds of explicit backlog. Neighbor ignition, thermal weakening, airflow and world-scale realtime are not established by this short experiment.

**Metal/contact integration:** static friction now distinguishes the force-kick projection used to maintain sticking from actual sliding heat. `tangential_constraint_projection_loss_j` remains a numerical diagnostic; physical energy residuals are still checked without subtracting it. Coulomb-limited or incoming sliding still dissipates energy. Final normal projection also checks for preexisting closing motion so a preceding contact's residual is not automatically called a support reaction. Native recordings and compact LLM evidence expose these terms separately.

Five contact tests cover analytic driven rolling, initial sliding, Coulomb saturation, unforced rolling and impulse transfer into a deformable face. The driven rolling oracle yields 17.858 m/s2 acceleration and approximately 2.78e-17 m/s contact slip; no arbitrary velocity assignment sustains rolling. These tests complement the existing glass/oak/iron impact regression. A small-scale contact correction is not unloaded-dent or glass-fracture acceptance.
