# Banjo roadmap and acceptance gates

**A cart that drives itself to a lake's edge, September 22 (source, branch `agent/cart-drive`):** [Milestone 2, its first step](machine-world.md#milestone-2-its-first-step-a-cart-that-stops-at-the-waters-edge). A controller can carry sensors now: a point on one of its machine's parts that reads the world, a depth, and the direction it stops the machine going. The first kind reads the depth of the room's water under the point; deeper than its depth, the controller will not drive that way, brakes, and says why ("water ahead: it stopped at the water's edge"), while the other way stays free. Rooms of exact bodies take machines: a battery in an exact body and a motor on a pin between exact bodies, and a saved world keeps their motors and controllers, which the engine refused to carry before. A controller's parts are everything that turns on a pin through either of its motor's two things, so E on a cart's front wheels finds its panel. In `/world?scene=tests-cart` (`tools/build_cart_room.py`, which drives the cart before it writes the room), the Workshop's cart with a 24 V battery, a 20 N m motor on its back wheels and a water sensor 0.6 m ahead of its deck goes 6.0 m down a lake's shore in 5.2 s and stops with its front wheels 0.13 m short of the water; downhill the motor mostly holds it back (the battery gave 26 J, the motor's work was -83 J, and 110 J became heat). The page draws each sensor as a bead on a thread, blue while it reads less than its depth and amber once it sees more, and the panel gives its reading. Checked by `tests/cart_drive_tests.cpp`, `tests/cart_room_tests.py` and the browser journey `ACartDrivesItselfToTheWater`. Not built: the bump sensor, roaming, and finding a charging post and docking.

**Ice melts in the world, September 22 (source, branch `agent/melting`):** [Ice melts](thermochemistry.md#ice-melts). The demonstration heat model, now version 2, declares one transition: ice into liquid water at 273.15 K for 333.55 kJ/kg (handbook values, reference-derived). The latent heat is ice's and liquid water's reference energies apart, and `Model::validate` refuses a transition they disagree with. Melting has no rate: after a step's heat has arrived, a zone holding ice and warmer than its melting point melts exactly C (T - Tm) / L at constant internal energy, so ice never warms past 273.15 K while it has any; the layer melts first and the core comes to the surface behind it. The meltwater runs off at once, leaving the network as matter out, and the live world puts it in the column of the room's water under the ice (`Environment::addWater`, a new `added_m3` term in the water's ledger) or, in a room with no water, counts it run off across the floor. Ice has a mechanical law with no curves that names ice as its own load-bearing matter, so a block shrinks from every face by what melted, weighs what is left, and leaves the world "melted away" when nothing is; the room page's Heat panel reports the melt rate, what has melted and where the meltwater went, and says once when something starts melting fast. Ice cannot be at a warm room's temperature, so a body of it joins the network the moment it is in the world, at its melting point, and a room holding ice now has a network even when its scene declares no heat; its ice melts slowly from the air, sky and floor (0.24 g/s for a 200 mm cube). Heat no longer refuses a whole room because an exact body is in it: heating the exact body itself is refused by name, and the bodies of cells beside it take heat, so the Explore valley's ice block, beside the cart, melts under **B** on the room page (`/world?scene=explore`). A world saved under model version 1 brings its ice across at the same temperature, with its ledger re-based by the same amount, and saves now say which model version wrote them. Measured: 10 kW for 60 s on a 200 mm cube with the surroundings at the melting point melts 1.79883 kg, which is 600 kJ over 333.55 kJ/kg to one part in a billion, never above 273.15 K; glass, oak and iron cubes under the same heater warm and keep their size; a block floating in a closed basin melts 0.917 kg into the lake, which holds exactly 0.000917 m3 more; in the Explore valley, 30 kW for 60 s melted 5.41 kg of the 7.34 kg block, which is now 128 mm across, and its 5.41 kg of meltwater pooled downhill. Not done: freezing (nothing in the world is colder than ice), heat between ice and the water it floats in (the water has no temperature), and ice's strength changing with temperature. The 30-item count stays 1 complete, 26 partial, 3 planned.

Retained in the backlog, not yet placed in a lane:

- **Thermal expansion and more materials.** Expansion is not modelled. The heat-dependent mechanical laws cover oak, iron and concrete, and none yet exists for glass, aluminium, ceramic, rubber or ice.
- **Wet soil and ongoing erosion.** No infiltration, pore pressure, wet-soil weakening or sediment moving while anyone is in the valley. Dug ground is carried as a tally of sand and soil, not as physical loose material.
- **Spatial heat and atmosphere.** Bodies hold lumped or surface-and-core temperatures, and heat paths use bounding boxes. There is no resolved temperature field inside a body, no plume and no smoke transport.
- **Complete interaction cycles.** The profile system reaches drawing and loosing. Physical placement, general rotation, sliding and activation, blade profiles, reusable load-operate-unload cycles and pose help remain; [the interaction profiles](interaction-profiles.md) lists the components.
