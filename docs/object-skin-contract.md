# Physical cells and object skins

Owner direction, September 5, 2026: objects may be represented by voxels or sphere/particle samples and wrapped in a separate visual skin. Fracture must produce surfaces for the surviving pieces, including newly exposed interiors. This is an architecture decision and implementation plan; it does not claim that the live material-network renderer already supports continuous skins.

## Representation contract

The authoritative object is occupied matter, material identities, physical geometry, connections/interfaces, motion, damage and energy/phase history. Numerical samples may be voxels, particles or sphere proxies, selected by the solver. Large inactive objects may retain compact chunk or aggregate representations without allocating one active rigid body per sample. The skin is a derived render mesh, with material/texture coordinates and bindings to that state.

An intact body has a continuous outer skin wherever its geometry is continuous. Cavities, openings and material boundaries remain represented. Rigid motion transforms the existing mesh; deformation updates bound vertices. A connectivity or occupancy change rebuilds the affected surface topology. A collision proxy is a third, explicitly bounded representation: visible sharp edges and smooth surfaces must stay within a measured error of the physical contact geometry. A detailed mesh cannot supply a missing cutting/contact law.

An appearance-only skin has no additional mass, strength or energy. Tomato peel, bark, coatings or a glass shell that contributes mechanical or thermal behavior must be declared physical layers/interfaces, with their own matter and properties. Their visual surfaces are derived from those layers.

## Fracture and new surfaces

1. The physical solver accepts deformation, failed interfaces and any material removal. Surviving connected matter and its history determine fragments; the renderer never selects a destruction pattern.
2. Preserve unchanged exterior geometry and its material coordinates where possible. Expose both sides of each open failed interface, even when another load path keeps the overall object connected. Do not wait for a complete split before showing a partial cut.
3. Assign surfaces to the accepted surviving components. Derive new interior faces from the materials actually exposed: wood grain/cross-sections, glass fracture surfaces or tissue interiors. A heterogeneous object can expose several materials on one fragment.
4. Each fragment inherits stable cell identities and the accepted material, damage, temperature/phase and motion state. Reskinning does not reset fracture history, recreate matter, apply an impulse or consume/release physical energy. Any actual new fracture surface energy belongs to the physical fracture law and must be accounted once there.
5. Smooth and shade only within the accepted topology and geometric tolerance. Never join detached fragments, cap an intentional opening, erase a cavity, bridge a crack, or visually regrow removed material. Newly created physical surfaces may require contact and heat-boundary updates owned by their respective solvers.

## Bounded execution

Use local surface patches with a small neighborhood overlap so an impact does not remesh an entire world or large object. Queue work using object/component identity, geometry/topology revision, material revision and visual detail level. Reject results for superseded topology. Vertices remain bound to the appropriate material coordinates and current component transforms so rigid motion does not invalidate every mesh job.

Prioritize visible surfaces and nearby damaged regions. Bound CPU work, queued bytes, triangle counts and GPU uploads; report mesh lag separately from simulation lag. A cheap exposed-face or cell view of the accepted geometry is the immediate fallback while a smoother skin is built. A stale intact skin must not bridge fragments that physics has already separated. No LLM or image generation runs per fracture. Appearance authoring may happen offline; geometry after an interaction is generated by the runtime.

Skin detail and physical resolution are independent settings with a declared geometric/contact error envelope. Lower visual detail must not change mass, inertia, forces, heat, damage or fragment membership. Rendering color/frost/wetness from temperature or phase does not implement liquid flow or volume change; those physical laws remain separate.

## Existing implementation and next slice

`src/fracture/FragmentGeometry.cpp::buildExposedVoxelSurface` already emits exposed cube faces for a supplied legacy voxel component. Its neighboring-cell culling uses reference-grid membership; it is not a general deformed skin or partial-crack mesher. Existing tests in `tests/test_main.cpp` check the ten exposed faces of two joined voxels and that fragment surface data is produced. These tests do not certify smooth or crack-aware skins.

`src/platform/NetworkWorld.cpp::renderInstances` currently exposes individual material cells, with explicit mesh data for rigid wedge tools. The live v2 network does not yet attach one skin to each deforming or separated component. `SparseThermalWorld` remains an independent field prototype; adding a skin does not couple its energy state to the mechanical backend.

The next implementation slice is a renderer-independent surface contract carrying stable component/cell IDs, exposed interface IDs, material coordinates and geometry revisions. First connect a simple blocky skin to accepted live connectivity, partial cuts and repeated fracture. Then add deforming/smoother surfaces, persistent buffers, local updates and bounded uploads. Preserve the cell/bond debug view alongside the skin view. This work can proceed alongside the unresolved glass/contact solver and thermal-frontier work; it must not conceal their accuracy limits.

## Acceptance cases

- Retain glass, oak and iron in identical declared shape/drop conditions. Adding tissue or another substance expands the matrix.
- Two joined cubes hide their shared interior; a failed interface exposes both sides. A partial crack remains open even if the object is connected around it.
- A sphere/ellipsoid retains an outer surface, then gains fresh interior surfaces on accepted fracture. Repeated impact creates further surfaces without restoring old material.
- A cut-out cavity and a layered wood/metal object preserve their openings and exposed material identities through fragment motion and remeshing.
- Visual detail, skin enable/disable and material renaming leave authoritative physical trajectories and ledgers unchanged.
- Under many simultaneous breaks, measure surface update p95/p99, memory/upload cost, fallback use and mesh backlog separately from physics. Reject stale asynchronous results and verify seams between updated patches.
