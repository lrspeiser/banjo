Model and remaining work

One native world owns the ball, sheet, posts and floor. Gravity and collisions drive all motion. Rigid baseline objects stay intact. The deformable experiment uses 72 sheet cells and 32 occupied ball cells connected by axial material bonds. The visible spheres are the actual collision shapes, with gaps between them; represented material volume determines mass.

Deformable stiffness requires very small substeps and is currently much slower than real time. A declared 14 m/s contact experiment skips the fall by starting with that velocity; it does not claim to simulate the preceding 10 m drop.

Glass uses the existing brittle law; metals use axial plasticity without fracture. Oak is an isotropic laboratory comparison without grain; rubber is not hyperelastic. Cell-frame bending, continuum calibration, contact/time refinement and a complete support/contact/damping/numerical energy ledger remain open. No precut shards or scripted fragment motion.
