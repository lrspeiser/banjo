          What happened

            The machine as data

            The base declaration, before your tray pieces are added. Edit it and build; the server checks it and the engine calculates the rest. Tray pieces are saved separately with the design.

              How honest is each station?

              Calculated uses the engine's tested laws for rigid bodies, hinges and DC circuits. Experimental is calculated but not calibrated against real material tests: oak burning and its loss of strength, and how glass breaks (whether it breaks is computed; the number of pieces is not calibrated). Not built is not in this shared world yet.

              Breaking can take the engine longer than real time; the clock then shows how far world time is behind.

              How to play

              1. Choose a challenge. Start with Level 1: Mechanics. Electricity, water and combined machines come later. Every challenge stays available, without filters.
              2. Read the task. The name and goal stay beside the scene. Green boxes mark destinations.
              3. Add a piece. Choose its pictured tray card. Once it is placed, click the actual piece to edit it. Used cards leave the tray. Sandbox has a full Add a piece library.
              4. Move or turn. The small toolbar beside the selected piece has Move and Turn. Choose one, then drag the piece with your mouse or finger. A click does not relocate it. Drag empty space to change the view; two fingers or a right/middle drag also work.
              5. Change a property. Choose the property beside the piece, then tap or drag its large gold handles. The arrows and live preview show the proposed change. Tap a choice to see its named options. Exact values and sliders are a fallback in Controls → Settings → Precision controls.
              6. Place and try. Place beside the piece confirms it. Try It! also places it before starting. A red piece must be moved until it fits. If the machine misses its goal, choose Try again: your setup returns to time zero and the last edited piece is selected. Reset starts a challenge over; Undo can recover your pieces.
              7. Change the view. Turn and tilt buttons sit above the zoom slider and magnifiers. Wheel or trackpad pinch zooms; two fingers turn and zoom on a phone. Undo and Redo stay in the scene corner.
              8. Draw wires. Click one connection ring, then the other. Match positive and negative terminals and complete the return path. Click a placed wire to remove it, then draw a replacement; Undo recovers a mistake. Flow arrows appear only when the engine measures current.
              9. Help and settings. Ask your question in Help. After a missed goal, Ask for Help sends your attempt to the assistant and opens a reply you can dismiss. Report a bug is in Help. Controls → Settings contains exact controls, snap preferences, level instructions and Share. Both snaps start on; Alt-drag temporarily bypasses them. Sandbox Save version keeps a design you can reopen.

              Keyboard: arrows move a piece; Page Up/Down change its height; Q/E turn, R/F tip and Z/C roll. Enter sets it down. Esc switches between moving and looking around. Ctrl+Z undoes a change.

Exact values and movement arrows still let you place between the lines, and saved designs keep their original positions.

              Updates right here

              Open Recent Changes from any game screen to see what’s new. Close it to return to your level or design. All the top links now fit on small phones.

              See what sets off Chain reaction

              Inspect switch takes you under the seesaw to see its two metal contacts. Watch the ball push them together, then follow the red and blue wires from the battery to the cannon’s igniter. Readings show whether the switch is open or closed and how much power reaches the igniter. Your result stays beside the game while you inspect.

              Clearer sunlight and power connections

              Catch the sun has a bigger wall and clearer shadows. Move the solar panel into sunlight, aim it, then connect its Out to the battery or winch’s In. The power meter tells you when the panel is shaded or its output is unwired.
