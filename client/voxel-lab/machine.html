How to play

  1. Each level has a goal: something has to end up in the green box in time.
  2. Press a piece's button at the top of the scene (+ plank). A blue ghost of it appears in the scene, ready to move.
  3. Drag it to move it (on a phone, one finger moves it and two fingers turn the view), tap where it should go, or use the arrows over the scene: they move it left, right, away from you and toward you as you see it, and up and down. Change and turn it with the controls under the scene (or Q/E, R/F, Z/C). Coloured lines through it show where it lines up. A red ghost cannot go there, and says why.
  4. Press Set it down, then Run it. To change a piece afterwards, click (tap) it in the scene. Nothing is animated: the physics engine calculates every bounce, burn and cut live.
  5. Win on as little energy as you can: the energy to make your pieces (smaller, lighter, cheaper materials) and what the run burns or draws. Beat a level's par for three stars, and play it again to do better.
  6. Stuck? Ask for a hint, or Place it for me (worth one star at most).

              How honest is each station?

              Calculated uses the engine's tested laws for rigid bodies, hinges and DC circuits. Experimental is calculated but not calibrated against real material tests: oak burning and its loss of strength, and how glass breaks (whether it breaks is computed; the number of pieces is not calibrated). Not built is not in this shared world yet.

              Breaking can take the engine longer than real time; the clock then shows how far world time is behind.
