            What happened

              The machine as data

              The base declaration, before your tray pieces are added. Edit it and build; the server checks it and the engine calculates the rest. Tray pieces are saved separately with the design.

                How honest is each station?

                Calculated uses the engine's tested laws for rigid bodies, hinges and DC circuits. Experimental is calculated but not calibrated against real material tests: oak burning and its loss of strength, and how glass breaks (whether it breaks is computed; the number of pieces is not calibrated). Not built is not in this shared world yet.

                Breaking can take the engine longer than real time; the clock then shows how far world time is behind.

                Share your course

                Friends can run this version. They can copy it to their own Sandbox to edit.

                How to play

                1. Choose a challenge. Start with Level 1: Wall Basics for left/right and up/down placement, simple circuits and water. Level 2: 3D Mechanics introduces depth; later chapters explore more electricity, water and combined machines. Every challenge stays available, without filters.
                2. Read the task. The name and goal stay beside the scene. Green boxes mark destinations.
                3. Add a piece. Choose its pictured tray card. Once it is placed, click the actual piece to edit it. Used cards leave the tray. Sandbox has a full Add a piece library.
                4. Move or turn. The small toolbar beside the selected piece has Move and Turn. Choose one, then drag the piece with your mouse or finger. A click does not relocate it. Drag empty space to change the view; two fingers or a right/middle drag also work.
                5. Change a property. Choose the property beside the piece, then tap or drag its large gold handles. The arrows and live preview show the proposed change. Tap a choice to see its named options. Exact values and sliders are a fallback in Controls → Settings → Precision controls.
                6. Place and try. Place beside the piece confirms it. Run in Build ▸ Tune ▸ Run also places it before starting. A red piece must be moved until it fits. If the machine misses its goal, choose Try again: your setup returns to time zero and the last edited piece is selected. Reset starts a challenge over; Undo can recover your pieces.
                7. Change the view. Turn and tilt buttons sit above the zoom slider and magnifiers. Wheel or trackpad pinch zooms; two fingers turn and zoom on a phone. Undo and Redo stay in the scene corner.
                8. Draw wires. Click one connection ring, then the other. Match positive and negative terminals and complete the return path. Click a placed wire to remove it, then draw a replacement; Undo recovers a mistake. Flow arrows appear only when the engine measures current.
                9. Help and settings. Ask your question in Help. After a missed goal, Ask for Help sends your attempt to the assistant and opens a reply you can dismiss. Report a bug is in Help. Changes → Settings contains exact controls, snap preferences and level instructions. The header’s Share link copies a link to your level or Sandbox setup. Both snaps start on; Alt-drag temporarily bypasses them. Sandbox Save version keeps a design you can reopen.

                Keyboard: arrows move a piece; Page Up/Down change its height; Q/E turn, R/F tip and Z/C roll. Enter sets it down. Esc switches between moving and looking around. Ctrl+Z undoes a change.

                Choose Sandbox → Pinball for an angled table with a spring launcher, powered flippers, contact buttons, bumpers and swinging targets. The test-site package checks the launcher and powered buttons before publishing the workshop.

                Wall Basics now has 26 lessons. Lift a marble in a motor-driven cradle, or use falling water to raise and unload it. Try air pushing a marble, steam raising a piston, a latched spring, a pendulum, a trapdoor, gears, hollow pipes, mirrors and lens focus. Learn resistance, stored charge, solar charging and parallel lamps on the same easy-to-place wall. The wall Sandbox has the new mounted tools too: find them under Add a piece → Wall mounted. Both marble lifts make one lift per Reset.

                Power wires fall, catch and pull free

                Bulb, switch, battery, heater and solar charging cords now join motor leads in reacting to gravity and solid objects. They settle as soon as you place the setup.                 More reliable wiring and cannon setups

                Motor leads have weight and can pull free

Too much load can pull a terminal free and stop the current; the cable’s edge label shows when that happens.

                Keep tilted panels clear of motor leads

                Sun-powered lift now uses the panel’s rotated shape when preparing nearby motor leads. Placing a tilted panel in clear space no longer blocks the power-connection controls.

                Adjust the spring after placing it

The spring’s stiffness stays fixed in this lesson; Sandbox has its separate stiffness setting.

                Swipe the camera row on small phones
