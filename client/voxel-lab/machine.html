          What happened

            The machine as data

            The base declaration, before your tray pieces are added. Edit it and build; the server checks it and the engine calculates the rest. Tray pieces are saved separately with the design.

              How honest is each station?

              Calculated uses the engine's tested laws for rigid bodies, hinges and DC circuits. Experimental is calculated but not calibrated against real material tests: oak burning and its loss of strength, and how glass breaks (whether it breaks is computed; the number of pieces is not calibrated). Not built is not in this shared world yet.

              Breaking can take the engine longer than real time; the clock then shows how far world time is behind.

              How to play

              1. Choose a challenge. Read its goal beside the scene. Green boxes mark destinations; other challenges measure things like light, power or water.
              2. Add a piece. Click the pictured card in Your tray, beside the scene controls. If there are several pieces, use its arrows to browse them. Browsing does not place a piece. In the Sandbox, Add a piece also opens the full piece library.
              3. Move and tweak it. Drag the blue ghost or use the arrows. A click or tap leaves it where it is and returns to View; dragging the ghost resumes Move. The small ground squares are 5 cm wide. Snap to grid gently aligns a drag near a grid line; turn it off for free placement, or hold Alt while dragging on a computer. Precision controls and movement arrows stay free. Drag empty space to turn the view, or use a right/middle mouse drag or Ctrl+drag even over the piece. On a phone, drag near the piece to move it and use two fingers to turn and zoom the view. Change its size and angle with the piece controls. A red ghost tells you why it cannot fit. Set down confirms your placement.
              4. Try your machine. Press Run it to place the piece you are adjusting and start the machine. A piece that does not fit stays red so you can move it. Pause stops the run; Set up again restores your setup. Tap a piece to move or change it. Solar collectors connect by clicking Output, then the receiving Input.
              5. Keep building. Beat a challenge's energy par for three stars. In the Sandbox, choose a prebuilt design or Add New Design, name your build and use Save version to keep a setup you can reopen.
              6. Draw wires. Open Readings and wiring in Controls, then Connect wires to show the endpoints on the pieces. Tap one endpoint, then another. Use + and − on batteries and bulbs, and In and Out on switches. A solar Out connects to a winch or battery In. Finish wiring or Esc hides the endpoints. Prepared wires and your wires follow the same floor routes; crossings do not connect. Yellow arrows show measured current. Open Flow preview for current numbers.
              7. Need a hand? Use Ask for help beside the game. Place it for me attempts a solution, worth one star at most.

              Keyboard: arrows move a piece; Page Up/Down change its height; Q/E turn, R/F tip and Z/C roll. Enter sets it down. Esc switches between moving and looking around. Ctrl+Z undoes a change.

              Recent Changes

              October 12, 2026

              Wires show current, and the marble closes metal contacts

              Wire highlights and moving dots now depend on measured current. An open switch or incomplete return loop keeps the leads quiet. Electrical previews start paused; choose Play preview to watch a rehearsal. In Marble turns on a light, the paddle carries an iron contact leaf that must touch a separate iron pad before the bulb gets current. Open Readings and wiring and choose Inspect contacts for a closer look. Earlier shared challenges keep their original switch.

              Fill the box before time runs out

              Fill the receiver now starts with an empty box. Collect 3 litres in 5 seconds: the source’s starting trickle is too slow, so increase tap opening. The meter counts water inside the box and shows time remaining while running. A missed deadline pauses the run; success shows how long the fill took. Preview water is labelled separately. Earlier shared challenges retain their original channel-collection setup.

              Clearer sunlight previews

              In Sun-powered lift and its Sandbox example, the bulb keeps its measured glow while its ray guides are hidden. The sun marker, panel meter and visible cable make the power source easier to follow. The sunlight connection guide stays with this lesson and its example.

              See how sunlight powers the lift

              Charge then lift is now called Sun-powered lift. A small sun-direction marker helps you aim the panel, and the controls show Sun → Panel → Battery → Motor → Arm. Set down the panel, click Panel Out, then Battery In to draw its charging cable. Panel watts, battery energy and motor watts show measured power, with previews labelled separately. The bulb is labelled as a shared load; Why the bulb? explains it when you want to read more. The same guide appears in the matching Sandbox example.

              A clearer river-powered lift

              Move the wheel into the marked river, then choose Connect rope to gate. The gate’s fixed guide and foot are no longer confusing connection choices in this lesson. The preview shows the wheel turning, the rope and gate moving, and measured wheel turn and lift. Run it starts the real machine; the result stays beside the scene.

              Divert water into a container

              Divert the water starts with a source and an empty container. Add a water chute, aim its lower end into the container and tip it downhill. Watch the water travel and the litres collected increase. Three litres must stay inside to finish. The win message stays beside the scene so you can see the water. Water chutes and separate collectors are also available in the Sandbox, with a ready-to-edit example.

              A simpler Banjo header and cleaner icon

              The header and browser tab now say Banjo. The red-and-white tab icon has transparent outer corners and no white border.

              Watch the laser burn through the cord

              Bounce the beam now shows the actual hot spot darkening, its temperature and the energy building toward material removal. Choose Inspect burn for a close-up. When the engine cuts through, the gap opens and the weight falls; the win message stays beside the scene so you can watch it happen. Whole machine restores your view. Reset clears the burn.

              A clearer burn close-up

              Burn inspection shows fewer beam lines so they don’t obscure the charred cord and the gap. The full beam still supplies the same power in the engine. Whole machine returns to the complete traced view.

              More work for your machine to do

              Turn the axle starts in reverse: choose forward to reach the target. River-powered lift starts on the bank with no drum connection: move the wheel into the current and connect it to the gate. Charge then lift and its sandbox example need a visible cable from the panel’s Out to the battery’s In. Water and electrical previews show Updating… immediately after an edit, so old readings are not presented as the new result. Earlier shared challenges keep their original setups.

              Choose a bulb your supply can power

              Light the bulb starts with a large 12 V, 12 W bulb on a small supply. Its label shows its rating and the volts and watts reaching it. Wire labels show the shared supply limit beside the measured flow. Pick the bulb again and reduce bulb size: the smaller bulb fits the supply and lights properly. Large, medium and small bulbs look different in the scene; your old saved bulb presets and shared challenges retain their original ratings.

              Power the lamp without balancing a battery

              Give it power now keeps the battery securely on the ground. Choose its voltage, then click the labelled + and − terminals to draw both wires. Start with thin wires and try a thicker pair: the lamp shows the voltage and power that actually arrive, so you can see why it is dim. The bulb needs 12 V for full brightness. Swapping its leads still works; swapping a motor’s leads reverses its direction. The battery-holder example and older shared challenges still reopen.

              See the power your machine needs

              Cables show watts and current as you adjust a design or run it, with moving dots showing flow. Powered objects have small labels for their rating, the level’s power target or an estimated starting power and torque for a simple lift. You can compare what arrives with what the load needs. Complex gear loads show the motor’s rating because their demand changes as they move. The labels stay close to the pieces and keep the run controls clear.

              Build with individual gears

              Gears now have visible teeth and come one at a time. Change their size, line up their axles and place the teeth together. They need a powered motor to move. In Turn the big wheel, choose and place a gear between the battery-driven wheel and the large target. A gap or the wrong size stops the transfer. The Fast wheel slow wheel sandbox example shows the same setup; older saved gear pairs still reopen.

              Start with something to work on

              Spring lifts the load opens with an oak load on a cradle and a green height mark. Add the spring beneath it and adjust its relaxed length. Raise the crate starts with the crate on its pedestal. Turn the big wheel starts with a powered gear and a target: size and place another gear between them. Pour onto the paddles starts with the wheel waiting for your tank. The river and receiver lessons show their working area before you add a piece. Your existing shared challenges and saved designs keep their original settings.

              A tank, a tap and a separate wheel

              Water tanks and wheels are separate pieces. Open the tap and aim the pour at the paddles. See the water inside the tank, with a small meter for litres remaining, tap opening and flow. A wider opening pours faster; the flow slows as the tank drains. Wheel in the river teaches moving a wheel off the bank and into the current, without a tank. Sandbox water examples use the same separate pieces; older saved designs keep their original feeds.

              See the light while you build

              Light paths stay visible while you move or adjust lenses and mirrors, with an updating label while the new path is calculated. Lenses have a clearer rim and a larger drag marker. Focus selected piece keeps the light source and target in view; Whole machine restores the overview. Lens placement and rotation no longer leave the scene empty because the glass was refused by its holder. Top navigation links wrap on the smallest phones so every link stays visible, and the inspector keeps enough space to reach its controls.

              Learn a bounce before bouncing around a wall

              Bounce to the target now comes just before Bounce the beam. Try aiming one mirror first, then take on the two-mirror challenge. Your saved stars stay with their levels.

              Easier to line up your pieces

              The ground has smaller, 5 cm squares. Drag a piece near a grid line and it gently aligns, with a small gold mark to show it. Snap to grid turns this help on or off. Exact values and movement arrows still let you place between the lines, and saved designs keep their original positions.

              Updates right here

              Open Recent Changes from any game screen to see what’s new. Close it to return to your level or design. All the top links now fit on small phones.

              See what sets off Chain reaction

              Inspect switch takes you under the seesaw to see its two metal contacts. Watch the ball push them together, then follow the red and blue wires from the battery to the cannon’s igniter. Readings show whether the switch is open or closed and how much power reaches the igniter. Your result stays beside the game while you inspect.

              Clearer sunlight and power connections

              Catch the sun has a bigger wall and clearer shadows. Move the solar panel into sunlight, aim it, then connect its Out to the battery or winch’s In. The power meter tells you when the panel is shaded or its output is unwired.
