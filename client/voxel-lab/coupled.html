Measured physics and calculation speed

Hosted testing uses the explicit native CPU reference. The local CUDA backend uses the same physical equations; saved scenes require their original backend and build. Neither backend is qualified for realtime sheet fracture.

Connected sheet: breaks under a 10 m drop, slowly. While glass bonds soften, a step must be shorter than about half a microsecond, or the cells' inertia cannot keep up and the solver stalls; the solver now finds that step. Oak and gentler glass impacts need more computing per step than the 30 s limit allows. A refused interval restores the last accepted scene. The separate ball / ground control demonstrates rebound. Compute pace excludes browser delivery. The CPU local Jacobian reuses unchanged contributions only within one private derivative batch; full CPU reference remains selectable in Solver settings.

Contact sites counts actual active quadrature sites in the last accepted substep, including supports. These are not cracks or removed voxels. Connected-sheet accuracy, fracture outcomes and realtime remain unqualified.

Model and work account

Independent rigid flight uses a swept separation bound to solve the sphere outside the material island. The sheet remains awake under gravity and supports. Before possible contact, the sphere returns to the shared solve with unchanged mass, poses, velocities and history. The ball still has no internal fracture model. Optional CPU thermal fields retain heat through flight/contact ownership changes. Reactions, flowing matter and damaged-fragment transfers remain unfinished.

Ball / ground is one density-derived ball and a fixed iron plane. Contact refinement bounds the sampled normal-contact phase when its velocity excitation exceeds 0.0001 m/s, and shortens a predicted free approach. This does not certify CCD, coupled material modes or full trajectory accuracy.

Nine connected 10 mm cubes on two fixed supports. Gravity, material traction and compliant contact solve together. Glass, oak and ice use declared mixed mode cohesive interfaces; iron uses a six mode plastic connector. Cell mass and inertia come from geometry and density.

Contact uses frictionless linear compliance. Box surfaces use face quadrature; edge collisions and continuous collision detection remain unqualified. Oak has no grain model; iron is not continuum J2 plasticity. Optional CPU conduction retains thermal fields on the same matter. Mechanical laws stay temperature-independent; melting and damage transfers refuse. No fluid laws here.

Every physical substep must pass momentum, angular momentum and energy gates. Failed intervals roll back and stop. The log includes actual intermediate poses, velocities, material history, external support reactions and numerical return excess. The latter is not heat.

The parallel Trial pipeline evaluates independent material/contact contributions and gathers them in reference order. Its tested low-energy trajectories match the serial reference exactly. Connected-sheet calculations remain much slower than realtime. Camera rendering is independent of calculations. No canned fracture motion or extrapolated physics.

Resident cuSOLVER keeps matrix error status on CUDA and uses reusable buffers. It is verified only on Windows x64 with the pinned NVIDIA library. The full nonlinear loop still waits on the CPU. CuPy reference stays the default; this option does not qualify realtime or more realistic material behavior.
